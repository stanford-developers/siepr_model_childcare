The Stanford Childcare Model

Comparing how different childcare policies affect families, providers, workers, and government spending.

The Challenge: Childcare is prohibitively expensive for many American families, yet providers operate on thin margins and childcare workers remain among the lowest-paid workers in the economy. Limited supply and high prices leave many families without access to formal care and can constrain parents’ ability to work.

The Policy Question: Policymakers are considering a wide range of reforms to make childcare more affordable and expand access. These include subsidies that reduce families’ out-of-pocket costs, tax credits, support for childcare workers, and policies that reduce providers’ costs. But these approaches can have very different effects—and very different fiscal costs.

What Our Model Does: The Stanford Childcare Model provides a framework for comparing these policy choices before they are enacted. It simulates how six proposed reforms affect families’ childcare choices, maternal employment, provider prices and wages, the supply of formal care, and government spending. By accounting for both the demand and supply sides of the childcare market, the model helps quantify the tradeoffs policymakers face when designing childcare policy.

The Stanford Childcare Model

How different childcare policies compare

The Stanford Childcare Model evaluates leading childcare policy options by estimating how families change their childcare and employment choices, and how providers respond through changes in prices, worker wages, and the supply of care.
